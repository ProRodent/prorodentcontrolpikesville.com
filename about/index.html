How a visit is scoped.

Every job starts with an inspection, not a quote over the phone. A technician checks the basement, attic, foundation line and exterior before recommending anything, because the right plan depends on where the animal is actually getting in, not just whether the house has mice or rats.

What we do not do.

We do not sell service based on a guess, and we do not leave a house with traps set and no plan to seal the entry points. We also do not use any rodenticide or exclusion material without walking the homeowner through where it was used.

Why the inspection comes before any treatment.

A guess over the phone cannot tell the difference between a mouse and a Norway rat, and the two call for different trap placement, different sealing materials and, in some cases, a different timeline. Skipping the inspection and jumping straight to setting traps or applying rodenticide means treating a guess rather than a confirmed problem, which wastes time and money if the guess turns out to be wrong.

The inspection also catches the entry point itself, not just the animal inside. A house can have every trap tripped and still have an open gap under the siding or around a utility line, which means the same problem comes back within weeks. Finding and closing that gap is what actually stops the cycle, and it only happens once someone has looked at the property directly.

Who we work with.

Most of our calls come from homeowners, but we take the same inspection-first approach for landlords, property managers and small business owners across our coverage area. A rental property or a commercial space has the same basic need, confirming what is active and sealing the actual entry point, even if the scheduling and documentation around it look a little different than a single-family home.
