Signs you have rodents

Check your home for these. Two or more usually means an active problem.

  • Droppings

    Dark pellets along baseboards, in drawers, under sinks or in the attic.

  • Gnaw marks

    Fresh gnawing leaves pale, rough wood on door frames, wiring and stored boxes.

  • Noises at night

    Scratching or scurrying in the basement, walls or ceiling after dark, more common once the weather cools.

  • Nesting material

    Shredded insulation, cardboard or leaf litter packed into hidden corners.

  • Rub marks

    Greasy smears along foundation walls, fence tops and pipes on regular routes.

  • Odors

    A musty, ammonia-like smell in closed rooms or near interior walls.

Rodent control for Baltimore County homes

Pikesville is an unincorporated community in Baltimore County, which means there is no separate town government here, just the county line and a ZIP code, 21208. Older houses and newer construction give rodents two very different kinds of gaps to work with, so the age and style of the house shapes where we look first.

Norway rats do well around older masonry foundations, where mortar joints and vent openings have had decades to wear, while mouse control calls come from nearly every house style once the weather turns. Both start with the same question: where is the animal actually getting in. We trace the travel routes first, then treat and seal, instead of just setting traps and hoping.

An older house often has different gaps than newer construction: aging roof vents versus tighter modern soffit returns, masonry foundations versus poured concrete. Tell us the age of the house when you call and we plan the visit around what that house style usually needs.

Questions about rodent control

How can I tell if I have mice or rats?

Look for droppings, gnaw marks, greasy rub marks and night-time scratching in the basement or walls. Rat droppings are noticeably larger than mouse droppings, and Norway rats are more often heard at ground level than overhead.

How do rodents get into homes in Pikesville?

Mice can pass through gaps of about a quarter inch and rats through roughly half an inch, so foundation gaps, dryer vents, plumbing chases and garage door gaps are the common entry points we find on local houses.

Will traps alone solve the problem?

Trapping removes the animals already inside, but sealing the entry points is what stops new ones from arriving once the cooler months push activity indoors.

Is treatment safe around children and pets?

Tell the technician about every child and pet in the home, and we explain what was used and exactly where before we leave.

How quickly can someone come out?

Our line is answered around the clock and same-day visits are offered where the schedule allows.

What happens after the first visit?

We check the traps, confirm the seals are holding and advise on any cleanup the household needs to do.
