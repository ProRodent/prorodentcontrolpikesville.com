What commercial accounts need differently.

A business call around Woodholme Shopping Center or the offices along Reisterstown Road has different needs than a home: documentation for health inspections, monitoring that does not disrupt business hours, and discreet placement that customers and staff will not notice.

Delivery entrances, dumpster areas and storage rooms are the most common entry points for retail and restaurant locations, since those areas see the most door traffic and often back onto landscaping or dumpster corrals that give rodents cover.

Inspection and monitoring.

  • Delivery entrances, dumpster areas and storage rooms checked first.
  • Discreet monitoring stations placed to avoid customer-facing areas.
  • Written documentation for health inspection or landlord records.
A business needs a record of the work, not just the work itself.

Scheduling around business hours.

Treatment and monitoring visits are scheduled around operating hours wherever possible, before opening, after closing, or during a slow period, so the visit does not disrupt customers or staff.

Ongoing monitoring versus one-time calls.

Some businesses need a single response to an active problem; others want ongoing monitoring tied to health inspection cycles. We scope either honestly based on the type of business and what the inspection finds, rather than defaulting to a recurring contract that is not needed.

Types of businesses we work with.

Retail shops, restaurants, and offices around Pikesville and the nearby Baltimore County commercial corridors make up most of our commercial calls, each with slightly different priorities around documentation, discretion and scheduling.

Working with property managers and landlords.

Commercial leases often put rodent control responsibility on either the landlord or the tenant depending on the specific lease language, and we have worked with both sides of that arrangement. If you are not sure who is responsible for a given space, we can still schedule the inspection and provide documentation that either party can use to sort out responsibility afterward, rather than delaying the visit over that question.

After the visit.

What businesses receive.

  • A clear answer on whether ongoing monitoring makes sense.
  • Discreet placement that does not disrupt the customer-facing space.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.
