Where outdoor activity starts.

Norway rats burrow at ground level, and a yard backing onto woods or a drainage swale gives them exactly the cover they look for: dense ground cover, a steady water source and undisturbed soil to dig into.

Burrow holes near a woodpile, under a shed foundation, or along a fence line where vegetation is thick are usually the first sign, often well before any activity shows up inside the house.

What we check in the yard.

  • Burrow openings near sheds, woodpiles and fence lines.
  • Compost areas and bird feeder zones that draw rodents close to the house.
  • Dense hedges or ground cover touching the foundation.
  • Drainage areas and low spots that hold moisture.
Outdoor activity caught early rarely has to become an indoor problem.

Treating burrows and outdoor harborage.

Active burrows get treated and, where it makes sense, collapsed once the animals are cleared. Tamper-resistant bait stations are placed only where pets and children cannot reach them, and we talk through whether a woodpile, shed or hedge needs to move back from the foundation as a longer-term fix.

Keeping outdoor activity from becoming an indoor problem.

A yard with active burrows near the foundation is a strong predictor of an indoor call within weeks, since the animals are already that close. Treating the yard and checking the foundation line for gaps in the same visit is the most effective way to stop that progression.

Follow-up.

Signs the yard is clear.

  • No fresh burrow activity near the treated areas.
  • Outdoor bait stations undisturbed on the follow-up visit.
  • No new rub marks along fence lines or the foundation.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about backyard and outdoor rodent control

Does living next to woods or a stream mean more rodent pressure?

Yards backing onto wooded or water-adjacent green space do tend to see more outdoor rodent activity, since the cover and moisture support larger populations nearby.

Will treating the yard alone solve an indoor problem?

No. If rodents are already inside, the house itself needs inspection, trapping and sealing in addition to any yard work.

Is it safe to treat the yard with pets that go outside daily?

Yes, tamper-resistant stations are placed where pets cannot reach them, and we walk through exactly where they are placed before we leave.
