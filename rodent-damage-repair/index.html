What rodents actually damage.

Mice and Norway rats chew constantly, not out of aggression but because their teeth never stop growing. That shows up as gnawed wiring insulation, chewed HVAC ductwork, shredded attic or basement insulation, and sometimes drywall or baseboard damage around a nest site.

Chewed wiring is the most serious of these, since exposed wire can trip circuits, disable equipment or, in rare cases, create a fire hazard. We flag any wiring damage found during an inspection and recommend an electrician for anything beyond simple insulation patching.

What gets assessed.

  • Insulation in attics, crawl spaces and basements for chewing and nesting.
  • HVAC ducts for gnawed openings that leak conditioned air.
  • Drywall and baseboards near confirmed nest sites.
  • Wiring for exposed or chewed insulation, flagged for an electrician if needed.
Repair work happens after the problem is cleared, not while animals are still active.

Repairing versus replacing.

Lightly damaged insulation can sometimes be supplemented rather than fully replaced, while heavily nested or soiled insulation usually needs full replacement to fully resolve odor and contamination. We explain which applies to your situation and price it honestly before any work starts.

Why repair follows removal and sealing.

Repairing chewed material before the entry points are sealed means a new animal can simply damage the same repair again. Our repair work is scheduled after trapping and sealing are complete, so it actually holds.

A note on older Pikesville housing stock.

Homes with masonry or stone foundations built before the 1970s, common throughout Pikesville and the surrounding Baltimore County communities, tend to show a specific pattern of damage: insulation nesting concentrated near a foundation gap, rather than spread evenly through an attic. Knowing that pattern in advance helps a technician narrow down where to look first during the assessment, instead of checking every area with equal priority.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent damage repair

Do you repair electrical wiring yourselves?

No, we flag chewed wiring and recommend a licensed electrician for anything beyond simple insulation work; we do not want to leave you with unsafe electrical repairs.

Can repair happen at the same time as trapping?

The active problem needs to be cleared and entry points sealed first, so repairs are not immediately re-damaged. We can schedule both close together.

Will insurance cover rodent damage repair?

That depends on your policy and is a question for your insurer; we can provide documentation of what was found and repaired if you need it for a claim.

Repair work across Pikesville
