What an inspection covers.

An inspection is not a five-minute look in the basement. A technician walks the full exterior (foundation line, roof vents, garage door, utility penetrations) and the interior spaces most likely to show activity: basement, attic, kitchen and laundry baseboards, and any crawl space access.

The inspection also covers areas homeowners do not always think to check themselves: crawl spaces, attic access points, and the underside of decks or porches. Activity in any of these spots can be just as relevant to a complete picture as anything found inside the living space.

The goal is a full list of entry points and activity signs before any pricing is discussed, because a plan built on a partial look almost always misses something and leads to a repeat call a few weeks later.

What you get at the end.

  • A plain-language list of where activity was found and how it is getting in.
  • A written plan covering trapping, sealing and any cleanup needed.
  • A price for that specific plan, explained before work begins.
You should know the plan and the price before a single trap goes down.

Why the house style matters.

Older homes tend to have different gaps than newer construction: aging foundation vents and stone foundations versus tighter modern sill seals. Mentioning the age and style of the house when you call helps the technician know what to look for first.

Estimates for exclusion and repair.

If the inspection finds chewed wiring, damaged insulation or foundation gaps, that gets priced as part of the same estimate rather than discovered later and billed as a surprise. You decide what to approve before anything is scheduled.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.

  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.

Questions about rodent inspection and estimates

Is the inspection itself free?

Ask about current inspection pricing when you call; either way, you are quoted and told the price before any treatment or sealing work is scheduled.

How long does an inspection take?

A full exterior and interior walk usually takes under an hour, longer if the basement access is difficult or the property is large.
