Why the cleanup matters, not just the removal.

Trapping and sealing deal with the live animals and the entry points. Cleanup deals with what they left behind: droppings, urine-soaked insulation and shredded nesting material that does not go away on its own once the rodents are gone.

Dry rodent droppings and nesting material should never be swept or vacuumed, since that can send contaminated dust into the air. The CDC's guidance on cleaning up after rodents calls for wetting the area with disinfectant first, then wiping it up, a step a lot of DIY cleanup skips.

Attics and crawl spaces in older Pikesville homes.

In many older houses the attic and crawl space are rarely entered and can sit undisturbed for years. That means droppings and chewed insulation can build up over years without anyone noticing, especially in a crawl space that is rarely entered.

  • Insulation checked for nesting, tunneling and urine staining.
  • Droppings removed following CDC wet-cleanup guidance, never dry swept.
  • Affected surfaces disinfected after debris removal.
Dry droppings should be wetted down before they are ever touched, not swept.

What gets replaced versus cleaned.

Lightly soiled insulation in a small area can sometimes be cleaned in place, but heavily nested or urine-saturated insulation usually needs to be removed and replaced, since it holds odor and will not fully sanitize. We explain which applies to your space before any work starts.

When to schedule it.

Cleanup happens after the active rodent problem is cleared and the entry points are sealed, not before, since cleaning an attic or crawl space with rodents still coming and going just gets re-contaminated.

Questions about attic and crawl space rodent cleanup

Can I clean a small amount of droppings myself?

If you choose to, wet the area with disinfectant first and never dry sweep or vacuum. For anything beyond a very small amount, having it done professionally is safer.

Does insulation always need to be replaced?

Not always. Lightly affected insulation can sometimes be cleaned in place; heavily nested or urine-saturated insulation usually needs replacing, and we tell you which applies before quoting the work.

Is the smell gone after cleanup?

Cleanup addresses the source, but lingering odor in porous materials like insulation is one reason replacement is sometimes the better option over cleaning in place.
