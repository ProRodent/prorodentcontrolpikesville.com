Why Summit Park needs rodent control

  • Mid-century construction

    Ranch and split-level homes built in the postwar decades often have crawl spaces and low foundations with age-related gaps.

  • Established mature trees

    Decades-old shade trees on Summit Park's residential streets give rub marks and travel routes close to rooflines and gutters.

  • Dense residential blocks

    Closely spaced lots mean activity on one property's foundation or hedge line often shows up on a neighbor's call too.

Low-slung homes and their own gaps.

Ranch and split-level homes tend to sit lower to the ground than newer construction, often with a crawl space rather than a full basement, and that is what we expect to inspect on a Summit Park call when the house fits that description. Crawl space access doors, vents and the low foundation line are where we start on a Summit Park call, since decades of settling can open small gaps that were tight when the house was built.

Split-level homes add another wrinkle: the half-flight entry and garage-under-bedroom layout common in this style create extra foundation seams at the level changes, seams that are easy to miss in a quick look but are a real entry point for both mice and Norway rats.

Mature trees and rooflines.

Where mature shade trees stand close to a house, limbs can reach near or over the roofline. While Norway rats are the more common ground-level call here, a tree limb touching the roof can still give a rodent an alternate route up and in through a roof vent or soffit gap.

  • Crawl space vents and access doors checked for age-related gaps.
  • Split-level foundation seams checked at interior level changes.
  • Tree limbs touching or overhanging the roofline noted and flagged.
A split-level's extra foundation seams are easy to miss and easy for a rodent to use.

Sealing a crawl space properly.

Crawl space vents get hardware cloth rather than a simple screen patch, since a simple screen degrades and tears faster than a rodent needs to find the next gap. Our exclusion and entry point sealing work treats the crawl space perimeter as a priority on this kind of home, not an afterthought behind the main foundation line.

Where homes sit close together, we also mention when a neighboring property's hedge, woodpile or foundation gap looks like a likely source. Clearing one house without addressing a shared property line issue sometimes means the activity returns from next door.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about Summit Park

Do split-level homes have more entry points than a typical ranch?

They often do, since the half-flight entry and garage-under-bedroom layout create extra foundation seams at the level changes that a single-story ranch does not have.

Should I worry about a neighbor's woodpile or hedge?

If it is close to the shared property line, yes, it is worth mentioning. Clearing your own house without addressing a nearby source sometimes means activity returns from next door.

Do crawl space homes need different sealing than basement homes?

Yes, crawl space vents and access doors need hardware cloth and a tight-fitting door, priorities that differ somewhat from a full basement's sill plate and window well gaps.
